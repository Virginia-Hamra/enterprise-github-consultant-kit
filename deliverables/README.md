# Types of deliverables for advisors and consultants

In the context of the Enterprise GitHub Consultant Kit, deliverables refer to the tangible outputs or artifacts produced during the various phases of a project or engagement. These deliverables can take different forms depending on the phase and the specific activities being undertaken. Here are some common types of deliverables you might encounter:

1. **Documentation**: This includes any written materials that provide information about the project, such as project plans, requirements documents, design specifications, user manuals, and training materials.
2. **Code Artifacts**: These are the actual code files, scripts, or software components developed during the project. This could include source code, configuration files, and any other technical artifacts that are part of the software development process.
3. **Reports and Analysis**: These deliverables include any analytical reports, performance metrics, or assessments conducted during the project. This could involve security assessments, code quality reports, or any other analysis relevant to the project.
4. **Presentations and Workshops**: These are materials prepared for meetings, workshops, or presentations. This could include slide decks, demonstration scripts, or any other materials used to communicate project progress, findings, or recommendations to stakeholders.
5. **Project Management Artifacts**: These include project plans, timelines, risk registers, and other artifacts related to project management and coordination. These deliverables help ensure that the project stays on track and that all stakeholders are informed about the project's status and any potential risks or issues.
6. **Training Materials**: These deliverables include any materials created to support training sessions or knowledge transfer activities. This could involve training manuals, e-learning modules, or hands-on lab exercises.
7. **Configuration and Deployment Artifacts**: These are the files and scripts necessary for configuring and deploying the software or systems developed during the project. This could include deployment scripts, infrastructure as code templates, or configuration files for various environments.
8. **Compliance and Governance Artifacts**: These deliverables include any documentation or evidence related to compliance with regulatory requirements, security policies, or governance frameworks. This could involve audit logs, compliance reports, or policy documentation.
9. **Customer-Facing Deliverables**: These are materials specifically created for the client or customer, such as engagement charters, success criteria documents, and business review templates. These deliverables help ensure that the client is aligned with the project's goals and outcomes.
10. **Knowledge Bases and Spaces**: These are curated collections of information, documentation, and resources that provide context and guidance for specific teams or projects. Knowledge bases are searchable repositories of information, while spaces are working contexts with pinned files and instructions to guide AI interactions.

Each of these deliverables serves a specific purpose in the overall project lifecycle and contributes to the successful execution and completion of the project. The specific deliverables produced will depend on the nature of the project, the needs of the stakeholders, and the goals of the engagement.
