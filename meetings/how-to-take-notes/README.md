# How to Take Notes

Notes are the **single most leveraged artifact** of a consulting engagement. Good notes make decisions defensible, hand-offs clean, audits painless, and customer trust durable. Bad notes lose decisions, repeat questions, and quietly burn credibility.

This guide is opinionated. Read in order:

1. [`principles.md`](principles.md) — what notes are *for*, and the mindset behind them
2. [`capture-techniques.md`](capture-techniques.md) — how to capture in real time without falling behind
3. [`decisions-and-actions.md`](decisions-and-actions.md) — how to write decisions and actions that hold up weeks later
4. [`templates-and-tags.md`](templates-and-tags.md) — filename, frontmatter, and tag conventions
5. [`recap-and-followup.md`](recap-and-followup.md) — what to do in the 24h after a meeting
6. [`anti-patterns.md`](anti-patterns.md) — what bad notes look like and how to avoid them

> **TL;DR**
> - Capture **decisions, rationale, actions, risks, open questions** — not transcripts.
> - Use the customer's words. Quote them when stakes are high.
> - Write actions in the meeting; confirm them aloud before closing.
> - Send a recap within 24h. The recap is the source of truth.
> - One file per meeting, named `YYYY-MM-DD-<short-title>.md`.
